when we are studying a patient with a hereditary disease.
we know that the disease might be caused by a mutation in a particular gene.
eg: BRCA1, a gene associated with hereditary breast and ovarian cancer.
we already know that DNA contains genes and that changes in DNA sequences are called variants.
But here's the problem:
Not every DNA variant causes disease.
Some variants are harmless. Some are pathogenic. And for others, scientists simply don't have enough evidence.
These uncertain variants are called *Variants of Uncertain Significance (VUS)*.
Three possible classifications 
- Benign
-Not disease-causing
- Uncertain
-Insufficient evidence
- Pathogenic
-Disease-causing
## ClinVar
A database containing genetic variants and their reported clinical interpretations.
For example, it can tell us whether a particular BRCA1 variant has been classified as pathogenic, benign, or uncertain.
##  what is MYH7?
The MYH7 gene provides instructions for making a protein called beta-myosin heavy chain, which helps heart muscle contract.
Some disease associated variants in MYH7 are linked to inherited heart conditions, including:
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): the heart muscle becomes abnormally thick.
-	Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM): the heart's pumping chamber can enlarge and weaken.
